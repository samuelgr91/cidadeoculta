# OP-061 — The nearly 250-year-old floor hidden beneath concrete

For years, anyone walking along the side of the Church of Bom Jesus de Matozinhos, São Miguel e Almas saw only a concrete surface.

Nothing there clearly suggested that something much older survived immediately below it.

That changed on 18 June 2025.

The church, in Ouro Preto's Cabeças neighbourhood, was undergoing a major restoration when workers began removing concrete from the right-hand exterior side of the building.

Old stone slabs appeared beneath the newer layer.

They were not merely remains from an earlier modern renovation.

According to archaeologist Maíra Mendes, in an explanation published by the Ouro Preto municipal government, this was the original floor from the period when the church was founded.

Part of the building's history had remained literally hidden beneath the feet of passers-by.

## An eighteenth-century church

The Church of Bom Jesus de Matozinhos and São Miguel e Almas was built between 1771 and 1793. It is dedicated to several devotions and belongs to Ouro Preto's historic religious ensemble.

Its façade also preserves a well-known detail: the sculpture of Saint Michael the Archangel above the doorway is attributed to Aleijadinho, although the municipal government notes that there are no sources that conclusively prove the attribution. Inside, paintings in the side corridors are attributed to Mestre Ataíde.

In 2025, however, it was neither a sculpture nor a painting that attracted the archaeologists' attention.

It was the ground.

## What was hidden

At some point after the original construction, the old exterior slabs were covered with concrete.

The modern layer concealed the earlier floor.

When the concrete was removed during restoration, the stones reappeared.

The municipal government described the find as the original floor along the right side of the church and reported that the slabs date to the building's founding period.

The stones therefore help reveal what the immediate surroundings of the church looked like before later interventions changed the site.

This is not a modern reconstruction made to resemble the past.

These are old elements that survived in their original setting.

## They chose not to hide it again

After the discovery, the decision was made to preserve the pieces.

According to archaeologist Maíra Mendes, the historic stones would be incorporated into the revitalised exterior and left visible. Where the floor needed to be completed, new slabs of the same local type, known as Ouro Preto stone, would be added.

That decision makes the find particularly interesting.

The aim was not to remove the stones and carry them to a museum.

It was to let them remain part of the place where they had survived for centuries.

## Why was the church under construction?

The discovery formed part of a much larger restoration.

The church had been closed for about a decade before the work began. The intervention started in the second half of 2024 with funding from Brazil's New PAC programme and municipal resources. It included structural work, roof restoration, new installations, fire protection and improvements to the exterior.

When the floor was found in June 2025, the municipal government reported that the restoration was approximately fifty per cent complete.

In September 2026, the city's latest update said that the first civil-works phase was approaching completion in November. The church would remain closed for the following stages devoted to its integrated artistic elements.

## What the discovery reveals

At first sight, it may look like nothing more than an old floor.

Finds like this matter because they show something recent photographs cannot: how a place was built and used before later alterations.

The stones also help archaeologists and conservators distinguish the historic building fabric from later additions.

In this case, the modern intervention produced an intriguing reversal.

Restoring the church required removing a newer layer in order to rediscover an older one.

## History was beneath their feet

Perhaps the most striking part of the discovery is its simplicity.

No secret room was found.

No treasure appeared.

No unknown tunnel emerged.

It was only a floor.

Yet that floor had survived for roughly two centuries, been covered in concrete and remained in place until a recent restoration revealed it again.

For years, residents and visitors passed the spot without realising that stones from the church's earliest period survived only a few centimetres below.

It is a good example of how Ouro Preto can still yield discoveries even in buildings that have been studied for decades.

Sometimes what the city hides is not behind a wall.

It is simply underneath it.

## What is documented

- The floor was discovered during the restoration of the Church of Bom Jesus de Matozinhos, São Miguel e Almas in June 2025.
- It was found outside, along the right side of the church.
- The old stone slabs had been covered by concrete.
- The archaeologist identified them as belonging to the church's founding period.
- The church was built between 1771 and 1793.
- The old stones were to be preserved and kept visible in the restored exterior.
- The current restoration began in 2024.

## What should not be claimed

The sources consulted do not support claims that:

- the floor was found inside the church;
- something lies buried beneath the slabs;
- the stones concealed graves, treasure or passages;
- no one knew of the floor before the intervention;
- every stone has occupied exactly the same position since the eighteenth century.

The documented discovery is compelling without invented mystery.
