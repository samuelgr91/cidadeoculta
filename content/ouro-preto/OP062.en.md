# OP-062 — The hidden paintings of Casa dos Contos

Anyone looking at Casa dos Contos from the street can tell that this is no ordinary building.

The mansion dominates Rua São José with tall windows, exposed stone, solemn proportions and the unmistakable presence of a place that has stood at the centre of Ouro Preto for more than two centuries.

Yet part of its beauty remained invisible for a long time.

During a complete restoration, paintings were found beneath ceilings and on walls. The work also revealed structures and architectural details that had disappeared under later alterations.

The building had preserved more than documents and memories.

Its own layers still concealed part of its history.

## Before the discovery, a house of power

Casa dos Contos was built between 1782 and 1784 as the residence and contractors' house of João Rodrigues de Macedo, who held tax collection contracts in the captaincy.

It was a prestigious private residence in Vila Rica. Macedo's social position and his connection to the fiscal system help explain the scale and monumentality of the building.

In 1789, during the repression of the Minas Conspiracy, the property quartered the viceroy's troops and served as a privileged prison for conspirators. Among those held there was the poet Cláudio Manoel da Costa, who died in the building.

From 1792 onward, the house accommodated the Public Administration and Accounting Office of the Captaincy of Minas Gerais. That role established the name by which the building is still known.

In the early nineteenth century, additions associated with the Assay and Mint House were made to the complex. During the twentieth century, several public agencies also occupied it.

## What the restoration found

The official page of Ouro Preto's Municipal Department of Culture and Tourism does not describe a single, monumental artwork.

It says that several paintings were found beneath ceilings and on walls, together with structures and architectural details that had previously been lost.

That precision matters.

There is no basis for claiming that restorers found a secret room, a mural deliberately sealed away or one specific masterpiece hidden by a particular person. What is documented is the discovery of paintings and architectural elements that were no longer visible before the restoration.

Even so, the find changes how the mansion can be understood.

Part of the decoration and physical organisation of its rooms had survived beneath later layers. The building had to be carefully opened and examined before those traces could appear again.

## A building can also be an archive

Historic houses do not remain frozen in time.

They receive new coats of paint, partitions, ceilings, utilities, repairs and adaptations. Each generation solves different problems and leaves another layer on the building.

Those changes can protect older elements, but they can also hide them.

At Casa dos Contos, walls and ceilings acted as a material archive. Instead of pages, they preserved pigments, forms and construction solutions. When later layers were examined, parts of that archive resurfaced.

The value of the discovery lies not only in the beauty of the paintings. It also lies in the questions they make possible: how were these rooms conceived, decorated and transformed over time?

## More than a cosmetic renovation

The restoration did more than return an old appearance to the building.

According to the official municipal page, the roof was reinforced while preserving the appearance of the original design associated with Macedo. Lightning rods, tensioning elements, fire extinguishers, walkways and roof lighting were also installed.

The project therefore had to reconcile conservation, safety and contemporary use.

It is during this kind of work, when surfaces are opened and structures are examined, that details hidden from view can emerge.

The restoration recovered the building and simultaneously produced new knowledge about it.

## Why the discovery carries historical weight

The paintings were found in a place directly connected to central themes in the history of Minas Gerais.

The mansion was the home of a tax contractor, housed troops during the investigation of the Minas Conspiracy, held prisoners and became the seat of the treasury administration.

For almost two hundred years, Casa dos Contos also accumulated records related to public administration and the regional economy. Parts of this archive were later divided among institutions including the National Library, the National Archive and the Minas Gerais Public Archive.

Today, the museum contains documentary and numismatic exhibitions, furniture, a library and other cultural collections.

The hidden paintings therefore carry a double significance: they are artistic remains, and they belong to a building that took part directly in the political, economic and administrative life of colonial Vila Rica.

## The surprise was in the walls

When people imagine a discovery inside an old mansion, they often think of safes, documents or underground passages.

At Casa dos Contos, the surprise was quieter.

No lost object appeared. Elements that had always belonged to the building became visible again.

The walls and ceilings ceased to be mere surfaces and became evidence.

That may be the most powerful part of this story: a famous monument could still keep a visible part of itself hidden in plain sight.

At Casa dos Contos, history was not only stored in the archives.

It was also hidden in the walls.

## What is documented

- Casa dos Contos was built between 1782 and 1784.
- It served as the residence and contractors' house of João Rodrigues de Macedo.
- In 1789, it quartered the viceroy's troops and served as a privileged prison for conspirators.
- From 1792 onward, it housed the Public Administration and Accounting Office of the Captaincy of Minas Gerais.
- The building underwent a complete restoration.
- During that process, paintings were found beneath ceilings and on walls.
- Structures and architectural details that had previously been lost were also recovered.
- The property has been listed by IPHAN since 1950.
- It now operates as Museu Casa dos Contos at 12 Rua São José.

## What should not be claimed

The sources consulted do not support claims that:

- a secret room was found;
- the paintings were deliberately hidden;
- the discovery occurred in a specifically identified room;
- all the paintings date to the eighteenth century;
- one single large mural was revealed;
- the building became haunted because of the discovery.

The documented account is already compelling: restoration revealed paintings beneath ceilings and on walls and recovered architectural details that had been invisible.
